The mission

A planar rocket booster has just separated. You are handed its full state and must compute the thrust and gimbal torque at every instant to bring it to the landing pad — centered at y = 0, spanning y ∈ [−20, 20] m — within 60 seconds. Your controller faces a fixed battery of 10 initial conditions of increasing difficulty (the same ten for everyone): higher altitudes, larger tilts, and faster spins as you go. Land all ten cleanly for a perfect run.

It is essentially an inverted pendulum on a cart — but the cart is flying, and the only way to steer sideways is to tip the whole vehicle and vector its thrust. Getting upright, killing horizontal drift, and touching down gently all at once is the whole game.

Anatomy of the booster

Every symbol in the equations below, in one picture. The kinematic state says where the rocket is and how it is oriented; the two inputs you command are the thrust magnitude and the gimbal torque. Distances and angles are drawn to a single scale.

A planar booster in the inertial frame (y horizontal, z up). The gimbal deflects the thrust by ψ off the body axis; because the thrust acts a distance L below the center of mass, that deflection is what rotates the whole vehicle.

y, z position (inertial) θ tilt from vertical  ·  ψ gimbal angle ẏ, ż velocity  ·  θ̇, ψ̇ rates (also state) fT thrust  ·  τ gimbal torque (inputs) m g weight (mass · gravity) L half-length (CoM→nozzle)  ·  J, JT inertias  ·  ve exhaust speed (sets fuel) zbottom = z − L cos θ height of the feet — this is what must reach the pad

The plant — rocket dynamics

State (constant mass): position (y, z), tilt from vertical θ, thrust-gimbal angle ψ, and their rates. You command two inputs each step: thrust fT and gimbal torque τ. The gimbal angle ψ is itself a state — τ slews it — so steering is a cascade.

Parameters

mass m
gravity g
half-length L
body inertia J = mL²/3
gimbal inertia JT
exhaust speed ve

Actuator limits

thrust fT
gimbal torque τ

Notes

This is the handout model with the thrust coefficient folded into fT (so it is a force in newtons) and the mass equation dropped — mass is held constant. Fuel is still tracked as ∫ fT/ve dt for scoring only. Integrated with RK4 at 50 Hz.

How the score works

At touchdown, six quantities are measured. Five of them are hard constraints: exceed any limit and that trial scores 0 (your rocket blows up). Fuel is not a hard limit — it just costs points. Within the limits, each trial earns

J = Σ αᵢ · (Mᵢ − |Pᵢ|) / Mᵢ (0 if any hard constraint is violated)
Pᵢ — measuredLimit MᵢWeight αᵢHard?
Fuel used150 kg10no — costs points
Horizontal miss |y|20 m30yes
Height of bottom off ground10 m20yes — must reach ground
Landing tilt |θ|π/6 (30°)20yes
Touchdown speed5 m/s10yes
Spin rate |θ̇|1 rad/s10yes

A perfect, zero-fuel landing scores 100. Across the 10-trial battery your total is the sum, so the maximum possible score is 1000. Take longer than 60 s and the trial is a timeout (0).

Writing your controller

  • Open the workbench and edit the controller in JavaScript or Python (toggle at the top of the editor). Anything goes — PID, LQR, MPC, gain-scheduling, switching logic. You get the full state every step.
  • Hit Test controller to fly all 10 trials. Watch the onboard camera and the superimposed trajectories on the mission map; set the playback speed to taste.
  • Your code auto-saves in this browser, and you can Download / Upload a controller file to back it up or work offline.
  • Happy with your total? Enter your name and @umich.edu email and Submit score. Only your best score is kept, so resubmitting can only help.
