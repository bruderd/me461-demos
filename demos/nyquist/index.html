How it works.  The Nyquist D-contour encircles the entire right-half s-plane clockwise: up the imaginary axis (with tiny rightward detours around any poles sitting on it, so they stay outside), then a semicircle of radius R → ∞ (dotted). Mapping this contour through L(s) gives the Nyquist plot. By the argument principle, the number of clockwise encirclements of −1, N, plus the open-loop RHP poles P, equals Z — the number of closed-loop poles in the right-half plane. The loop is stable when Z = 0. The Bode plot is exactly the positive-jω part of the contour (same maize color), so gain and phase margins are the distances to the −1 point read off in the frequency domain. Everything runs client-side.
