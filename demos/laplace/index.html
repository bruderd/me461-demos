How it works.  The Laplace transform F(s) = ∫₀∞ f(t) e−st dt turns a time signal into a complex-valued function of the complex frequency s = σ + jω. The surface plots |F(s)| as height and ∠F(s) as color (the ring on the right is the color key). Where f(t) is a sum of terms of the form tneat·{sin,cos}(ωt), F(s) is a rational function computed exactly — its poles are the spikes where |F| → ∞, and its region of convergence is the half-plane to the right of the rightmost pole, Re(s) > σa (shaded on the floor and in the s-plane). For any other f(t) the integral is evaluated numerically over [0, T]; it only converges to the right of the abscissa of convergence, so the surface there is a truncated approximation. Move the floor cursor (or drag in the s-plane) to read s and F(s) in rectangular and polar form. Nothing runs on a server.
