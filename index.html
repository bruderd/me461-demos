MECHENG 461

Automatic Controls — Interactive

Design controllers and watch dynamical systems respond, entirely in your browser. Nothing to install; nothing runs on a server.

Playgrounds · tune & watch live

Cruise Control over Hills →

Hold a target speed while the road grade fights back. Tune a PID or write your own controller.

Phase Portrait Explorer →

Type any 2-D system ẋ = f(x), read its vector field, then drop initial conditions and watch trajectories run in phase space and in time.

Linearization Explorer →

Run a nonlinear system and your linearization of it side by side from the same start. Compare in deviation coordinates x̃ or mapped back into x to see the local approximation hold — then break.

Complex Exponential est →

Place a pole s = σ + jω in the s-plane and watch the mode est it generates — a spiral that decays, sustains, or blows up. Drag it like a 2-D slider, read off ζ and ωₙ, and toggle the conjugate pole.

Sum of Complex Exponentials →

Build f(t) = Σ ckeskt from up to eight complex phasors and watch them chain tip-to-tail in the complex plane, tracing the real and imaginary parts of f(t) in time. See how a conjugate pair sums to a real cosine.

Laplace Transform F(s) →

Type a signal f(t) and see its transform F(s) rise as a 3-D surface over the complex plane — height is |F(s)|, color is its phase. Drag the s-cursor across the poles, read F(s) in rectangular and polar form, and watch the region of convergence.

Two-Compartment Drug Delivery →

Infuse a drug into the bloodstream and control its concentration in the heart. Write a control law u(c₂), watch the response track the target, the compartments fill with color as the drug flows, and read off settling time and overshoot.

Nyquist Stability Explorer →

Type a loop transfer function L(s), sweep the gain k, and map the D-contour through it. Move a cursor around the contour to link the Bode plot, the pole/zero map, and the Nyquist plot — then count encirclements of −1 to read closed-loop stability, Z = N + P.

Lyapunov Function Explorer →

Enter a 2-D system ẋ = f(x) and a candidate V(x). See V as a surface tinted green where V̇ < 0, drop an initial condition, and watch the trajectory slide downhill both in the phase plane and on the surface while V(x(t)) falls on the right.
